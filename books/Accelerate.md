## Summary

Four years of rigorous research examining how software delivery performance drives organizational success. The core question: does technology actually matter competitively? The answer: yes, and it's measurable.

### The Four Key Metrics (DORA)
1. **Lead time** — commit to production
2. **Deployment frequency** — how often you ship
3. **Mean time to restore (MTTR)** — how fast you recover from failure
4. **Change failure rate** — what percentage of changes cause incidents

### Key Findings
- **Continuous delivery and DevOps** significantly correlate with both speed AND stability (they are not trade-offs)
- **Loosely coupled architecture** enables team autonomy and faster delivery
- **Automated testing and trunk-based development** are essential technical practices
- **Quality must be built in**, not inspected afterward
- **Heavyweight change approval processes** (CABs) negatively impact delivery speed *without improving stability*
- **Generative culture** (learning from failures, not blame) is the biggest organizational predictor
- **Transformational leadership** (vision, intellectual stimulation, supportive management) measurably affects team performance
- **Communities of practice** outperform centers of excellence
- The biggest predictor of security practices is cultural, not technical

### Best Use Case
Most valuable for convincing executives with data-backed justification for DevOps/lean transformation. For practitioners already doing DevOps, it's validation rather than new tactics.

### Criticisms Worth Noting
- Repetitive and report-like writing style
- Limited novelty for those already deep in DevOps literature
- Survey methodology relies on self-evaluation
